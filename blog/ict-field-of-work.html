The ICT Field of Work: Digital Forensics at Politie Zeeland

18-10-2026 by Kasper van de Kimmenade

From August 2025 to January 2026, I did my MBO internship at Politie Zeeland in the digital forensics unit (Digitaal Opsporen). This gave me an inside look at how IT works in law enforcement.

The Problem with Phone Dumps

When the police seize a phone, they pull huge amounts of data from it using tools like Cellebrite or Graykey and more. For investigators doing deep database research (because GUIs do exist), going through that mountain of data is slow and messy. Apple and Android use completely different timestamp formats, GPS coordinates are just raw numbers instead of place names, and the existing software gets cluttered quickly.

Building the AI Query Tool

I built a proof of concept tool that lets investigators search through SQLite database dumps using normal language, like asking for photos taken in Middelburg during a certain month. The backend converted timestamps between iOS and Android formats, translated GPS coordinates into city names using Nominatim, and used an async FastAPI backend connected to Ollama and OpenWebUI.

Real World Rules

Working with forensic evidence taught me that legal requirements matter as much as the code itself. The tool had to use strictly read-only database connections so evidence was never altered, everything had to be logged, and real case data could never leave an isolated local network. It showed me how critical reliable backend engineering is in the public sector.
